When orders come, military homeowners in San Diego face a big decision fast. Many sellers I talk to right now are torn, especially those with a low interest rate they don't want to give up.

The case for renting it out

  • You keep your low rate. For many owners, their current mortgage payment is well below what it would cost to buy the same home today.
  • Strong rental demand. San Diego's large military population means steady demand from incoming families.
  • Long-term equity. Your tenants help pay down the loan while you're stationed elsewhere.

The case for selling

  • Restore your VA entitlement. Selling and paying off the loan can restore your full entitlement for $0-down buying at your next duty station.
  • No landlord stress from far away. Repairs, vacancies and tenant issues are real, even with a property manager.
  • Unlock your equity for your next down payment, debt payoff or savings.
  • Tax timing. The capital gains exclusion depends on owning and living in the home for 2 of the last 5 years. Military members on qualified extended duty may be able to suspend that clock. Ask a tax professional.

Questions to answer before you decide

  1. What would the home rent for, and what's my true monthly cash flow after management, maintenance and vacancy?
  2. Do I need my full VA entitlement to buy at my next base?
  3. Do I plan to return to San Diego?
  4. Am I comfortable being a long-distance landlord?
