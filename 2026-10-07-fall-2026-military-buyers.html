Every week I talk with service members and veterans trying to buy in San Diego. The questions are remarkably consistent, so here's what I'm hearing and what I'm telling them.

1. "Should I wait for rates to drop?"

This is the number one question. My honest answer: nobody can time rates. What you can control is buying a home that fits your budget today. If rates fall later, refinancing may be an option (VA has a streamlined refinance program called the IRRRL). If rates rise, you've locked in. Waiting has its own cost: rent payments, and potentially rising prices if more buyers return when rates ease.

2. Competition hasn't disappeared

Well-priced homes in popular military neighborhoods like Chula Vista, Santee and Oceanside can still draw multiple offers. Homes that need work or are overpriced tend to sit, which can create opportunity for patient buyers who are ready to negotiate.

3. VA offers are winning, when they're presented right

I still hear from buyers whose previous agent told them "sellers don't like VA." A clean VA offer with a strong local lender, a realistic appraisal plan and a clear timeline is a strong offer. I call listing agents before submitting to explain exactly why my VA buyer is a safe bet.

4. Assumable VA loans are the hidden opportunity

Homes with assumable low-rate VA loans can mean a much lower monthly payment, if you can cover the equity gap. These aren't always advertised. Read my guide to VA assumable homes.

5. PCS timing is everything

Buyers who start 60 to 90 days before their report date have the most leverage. Those who wait until they arrive often end up in TLA, under pressure. If you have orders for summer, now is the time to get pre-approved and start virtual tours.

The buyers winning right now aren't the ones with perfect timing. They're the ones with a plan.

My advice this fall

  • Get fully pre-approved, not just pre-qualified, with a VA-experienced lender.
  • Know your comfortable payment, not just your max approval.
  • Ask about seller credits for closing costs or a rate buydown.
  • Keep an eye out for assumable loans.
